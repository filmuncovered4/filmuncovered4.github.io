Note: There is one thing worth mentioning about the long-form work above. I do not only edit those videos. I research the subject, develop the theory, write the script, record the voiceover, make the thumbnail, and then edit everything myself. This means the editing is often limited by the amount of time I have left after doing everything else. I could spend considerably more time refining the pacing, sound design, motion graphics, compositing and smaller visual details if editing were the only thing I had to worry about. Which is, perhaps, a rather convenient thing to mention on an editor's portfolio. I enjoy building sequences around music, finding the right rhythm, designing sound, creating transitions, compositing shots and, when necessary, making something appear on screen that was never actually there. I also understand the storytelling side of editing because I have spent years doing the other parts myself. I know why a scene is there. I know what information the viewer is supposed to notice. I know when a story, or in my case a theory needs to breathe and when it needs to move. So while these videos show what I can do while simultaneously being the researcher, writer, voice, thumbnail designer and editor... I am rather curious what the editing would look like if I only had to be the editor.

About

More than
an editor.

I have been editing since I was young and have spent years studying film, television, YouTube storytelling and trailers as an art form.

My long-form work often starts with an unusual question. I research obscure real-world events, psychology, science and other ideas to find perspectives that are not immediately obvious, then build the story around them.

My trailer work is where I experiment most heavily with pacing, music, sound design, rotoscoping, compositing and creating scenes that did not exist in the original material.
